**Speaker notes:**

**COCOA SLIDE — SOURCING HYPOTHESIS, NOT CONFIRMED SUPPLY CHAIN**

- User's team discussed 67% as a product prototype. Six simulated/AI persona responses were interpreted as broadly favouring 35–65% and curious about sampling 65–75%. This does NOT demonstrate real consumer preference; confirm with a real tasting panel and final recipe testing.
- Propose evaluating one Indian origin, Karnataka, and one international origin, Ghana, not claiming an existing supplier or blend. Both need actual supplier names, farms/cooperatives, lot traceability, quality certificates and prices.
- Cocoa can be purchased as fermented, dried beans, not only powder. Bean-to-bar manufacture is a separate capability: cleaning, roasting, winnowing, grinding/refining, formulation, conching/tempering as applicable. Cocoa powder is produced by pressing cocoa liquor, then processing the cake, not the raw form.
- In-house processing affects capex, trained labour, electricity, yields, inventory, import rules, QC and actual cup cost; consider pilot contract processing versus full in-house investment.

## Slide 9

**03 · THE ATELIER · SOUND**

### The sound of sweetness

**THE CHALLENGE**

High-cocoa chocolate is naturally bitter, and monk fruit replaces much of the sugar. We want every cup to feel sweeter without adding sugar back.

**THE SCIENCE**

“Sonic seasoning” research from [Charles Spence](https://www.office.com/search?q=Charles+Spence&EntityRepresentationId=df680ae5-5cc0-8c4e-bcbf-ef1bf0c60b9c)’s Crossmodal Research Lab at Oxford shows that what we hear shifts how sweet or bitter food tastes.

**Tastes sweeter**

High pitch · smooth · harmonious · soft

**Tastes more bitter**

Low pitch · brassy · harsh · loud

**OUR ATELIER PLAYLIST**

- Santoor
- High bansuri
- Jal tarang
- Celesta
- Soft piano

*Played softly: loud background noise dulls sweetness.*

**Speaker notes:**  
High cocoa is bitter and monk fruit is less sweet than sugar. Crossmodal research shows high-pitched, smooth, harmonious music nudges perceived sweetness up, while low, brassy sound pushes it toward bitter. So the Atelier plays santoor, high bansuri, jal tarang, celesta and soft piano, at low volume. The effect is a nudge, not magic. [1](https://globalappsportal-my.sharepoint.com/personal/utsav_mehta_dentsu_com/_layouts/15/Doc.aspx?sourcedoc=%7BEAEDBB13-3D69-494B-8086-A30FBC83262E%7D&file=KATHAI_Campaign_Deck_V4.pptx&action=edit&mobileredirect=true)

### How prices shift demand across KATHAI

**Café PRICING 01/02**

### FIVE-STEP MODEL APPROACH

1. **Synthetic choice data:** 5,000 choice occasions
2. **Mixed Logit model:** Heterogeneous preferences
3. **Demand simulation:** Shares & elasticities
4. **Cannibalization:** Demand transfer mapping
5. **Price optimization:** Demand vs. profit objectives

### 1% PRICE INCREASE · EXPECTED CHOICE CHANGE

Columns = product repriced | Rows = affected SKU

| Affected SKU | Hazelnut | Milk | Rush-E | Salora |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Hazelnut Praline | −1.328% | +0.151% | +0.193% | +0.175% |
| Milk Chocolate | +0.209% | −1.371% | +0.205% | +0.186% |
| Rush-E | +0.201% | +0.154% | −1.271% | +0.177% |
| Salora | +0.206% | +0.158% | +0.200% | −1.278% |

Diagonal = own-demand decline · Off-diagonal = within-KATHAI substitution.

### 5% PRICE SHOCK · DEMAND TRANSFER

Expected changes in choice occasions:

| Price increase | Own loss | Other KATHAI | Competitors |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
